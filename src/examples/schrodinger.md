# Schrödinger Equation

## Equation

$$
\mark[imaginary]{i}\mark[planck]{\hbar} \mark[timederiv]{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}} \mark[wavefunction]{\psi(x,t)} = \mark[kinetic]{-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}} \mark[wavefunction]{\psi(x,t)} + \mark[potential]{V(x)} \mark[wavefunction]{\psi(x,t)}
$$

## Description

A [quantum wave]{.wavefunction} [rotates in phase]{.imaginary} [over time]{.timederiv}, with [the reduced Planck constant]{.planck} linking its rotation rate to energy: [kinetic energy from the wave’s curvature]{.kinetic}, plus [potential energy at each position]{.potential}.

## .imaginary

The imaginary unit $i = \sqrt{-1}$.

It makes each energy component of the wavefunction rotate in the complex plane. Components with different energies rotate at different rates, changing how they reinforce or cancel over time.

## .planck

The reduced Planck constant $\hbar = h/2\pi$.

It represents the fundamental quantum of action, setting the scale at which quantum effects become significant. It connects energy to frequency ($E = \hbar\omega$) and momentum to wavenumber ($p = \hbar k$).
**Value:** $\approx 1.055 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s.

## .timederiv

The rate of change of the wavefunction over time.

This derivative describes how the state evolves. The $i\hbar$ factor turns this rate of change into an energy term (the Hamiltonian acting on the state), linking time evolution directly to the system's total energy.

## .kinetic

The kinetic energy operator.

It measures the wave’s curvature: shorter-wavelength components have larger momentum magnitude and higher kinetic energy.

## .potential

The potential energy function $V(x)$.

This represents the environment the particle moves in, such as an electron in an electric field or a particle in a box.

## .wavefunction

The wavefunction $\psi(x,t)$, a complex-valued probability amplitude.

It contains all measurable information about the particle. The Born rule gives the probability density $|\psi(x,t)|^2$. Integrate it over a region to find the probability of finding the particle there.
